OCD — what it can do

OCD keeps your photos safe across your computer, your phone, and portable hard drives. It works without an internet connection, without an account, and it never deletes anything until it has checked that a good copy exists.

Works today Works, but not fully Not built yet

Tap “Technical details” under any item if you want the plumbing.

How it works

Every way of adding photos goes through the same four steps.

1Find the photosFrom your phone, a folder, or a camera.
2Check the copyReads every byte back to make sure it matches.
3Save and recordPuts them in a dated folder and writes a note of what happened.
4Your moveBrowse them, or clear space — only after the check passes.

On your computer

WORKS

One small program you run on Windows, Mac, or Linux. No installer and no account.

Technical details
Single Go binary, cross-platform builds, one-click launcher, CLI, self-update.
WORKS

Browse your drives in OCD’s own gallery, jump between years and months, mark favourites, and open a photo’s folder. Import, duplicates and help each have their own tab.

Technical details
Paged server-side index with thumbnail cache, year/month timeline, favourites, reveal in file manager, thumbnail export. Photofield is optional and only used if you open its own interface.
WORKS

Copy photos in from your phone over Wi-Fi, from a cable, or from a folder. The page is split by how the photos arrive, and every copy is checked before anything is cleared.

Technical details
Watched landing folder for Quick Share / Phone Link / the OCD app, plus folder / ADB / iPhone importers. SHA-256 verification, optional wipe of verified originals, content and name dedupe with quarantine. Name-only matching sits under “Advanced comparison”.
WORKS

Your phone can send photos to the computer over Wi-Fi, and the computer checks what arrives.

Technical details
Upload API with server-side hash check, staged in .ocd-incoming then moved into Phone/YYYY/MM/; import-check endpoint for on-drive badges.
WORKS

The drive keeps its own index, and the computer uses it instead of re-reading every file. A copy on the computer keeps the gallery working when the drive is unplugged.

Technical details
Scan reads .ocd/catalog.gz and adopts entries before walking, so unchanged files are not probed again. Writes a local mirror to <data>/catalog/<volume>.jsonl.gz, loads it at startup, and falls back to it if the drive catalog is damaged. GET /api/catalogs reports drive vs mirror.
NOT YET

Right now anyone on your Wi-Fi can open the computer’s photo pages. A pairing step with a code or QR will lock that down.

Technical details
Planned: bearer/cookie token auth on all /api/*, /api/pair/* public and rate-limited, loopback cookie for the desktop UI.
NOT YET

A list of every computer and phone that has used the drive, with when each was last seen.

Technical details
Planned: devices.json registry with heartbeat and last-seen counts.
NOT YET

Sending photos from the computer back down to the phone.

Technical details
Planned: desktop→phone send. The audit logs it would draw from already exist and are shown on the phone’s Activity screen.

On the drive

The drive carries a small index of what is on it, so another machine or a phone can understand it quickly.

WORKS

The drive remembers what is on it, even if you unplug it or its letter changes.

Technical details
.ocd/catalog.gz with header, volume ID, and one line per item. Written tmp → sync → rename, with catalog.bak fallback and header validation.
WORKS

Every add, move, and delete leaves a written record you can look back at.

Technical details
Append-only audit.jsonl on both the desktop upload path and the phone backup path; archived files stay restorable from the log.
WORKS

The drive is recognised by its hardware ID, not by its drive letter, so it survives being plugged in as E: one day and G: the next.

Technical details
Native volume serial per OS, with volume.json only as a fallback. Item IDs are volume ID + relative path.
WORKS

The computer keeps its own copy of the drive’s index, so the gallery still fills while the drive is unplugged.

Technical details
Local mirror at <data>/catalog/<volumeId>.jsonl.gz, loaded at startup and used as a fallback if the drive catalog is damaged. GET /api/catalogs reports drive vs mirror.
NOT YET

Two computers writing to the same drive at the same time are not yet coordinated, so they could still tread on each other’s index.

Technical details
Planned: .ocd/catalog.lock and a catalog journal, so concurrent writers are ordered rather than assumed away.

On your phone, over Wi-Fi

The phone talks to OCD running on your computer.

WORKS

Type in your computer’s address — the computer shows it with a QR code under Import — and browse its photos from the phone, including full-screen, zoom, video, and sharing.

Technical details
Connect screen, PC gallery, viewer with zoom, remote video, share sheet. The address and QR live in a collapsed block on the desktop Import page.
WORKS

See your camera roll with a badge on the photos that are already on the drive, filter to new ones, and send several at once.

Technical details
Camera-roll grid, on-drive badges from /api/import/check, “new only” filter, multi-select upload.
WORKS

The computer checks each upload, and only then can you delete the copy on your phone to free space.

Technical details
Camera-roll assets staged into app sandbox, server re-hashes, verified delete only on success.
WORKS

A dropped connection does not start the file again. The phone asks the computer how much already arrived and sends only the rest, even if the app was closed mid-file.

Technical details
Upload session saved in the queue before the first byte; GET /api/upload/{id} reports received; PUT ?offset=N appends; resumed sends use 4 MiB chunks; a different file size starts a fresh session instead of appending.
WORKS

Sending survives closing the app. The queue is kept on the phone, so an interrupted send is still waiting next time, and you can pause, resume, retry or clear it.

Technical details
On-disk queue (ocd-send-queue.json) with per-item pending/done/failed state; auto-resume once per app open; Stop finishes the current item; server re-hash idempotency makes retries safe. Still foreground-only.
WORKS

Free space in one place. The Space tab lists only the photos the computer has verified, shows the phone’s free space now and after the items you pick, and lets you delete them from the phone.

Technical details
Verified sends persist in ocd-verified.json with asset id, size, date and drive path. Space resolves presence against the camera roll, refuses catalog-only matches, confirms count and bytes, deletes via deleteAssetsAsync, then forgets the proof. A verified delete posts count and bytes to /api/free/record, which writes one op:"delete" row per folder. Missing items can be forgotten separately.
WORKS

One Photos grid for every source. The camera roll, a drive plugged into the phone, and a computer on the same Wi-Fi appear together, newest first. Chips narrow the grid to one source, and every tile knows where it came from.

Technical details
Three page loaders (camera roll, drive catalog, /api/photos) are merged by capture date with a linear merge. Keys are prefixed phone:, drive: and pc: so previews, originals, video and sharing route back to the right source. A source that is not available is left out of the chips rather than shown empty.
WORKS

Find the computer on your Wi-Fi. If you forget the address, Devices → Scan network asks the addresses around the one you last used and lists any that answer.

Technical details
Bounded /24 probe on port 8090, 254 hosts, 900 ms timeout, 20 at a time, only on an explicit tap. The saved address or a typed one supplies the subnet; public addresses and hostnames are refused. Every hit is confirmed by /api/version, so another service on the port is not mistaken for OCD.
WORKS

See what the app has done. An Activity screen lists recent sends, phone backups, freed phone space and duplicate moves, newest first.

Technical details
Backups tab shows queue counts and links to Activity. Activity merges each drive's .ocd/audit.jsonl plus the computer's log under DataDir/.ocd via /api/activity; tail read (2 MiB per log), 120 rows by default, 500 cap, read-only.
WORKS

Five plain tabs. Photos, Devices, Backups, Space and Settings. Each screen says what it is for, and anything that needs the computer says so instead of failing quietly.

Technical details
Five always-mounted screens with display:'none' when inactive, so a drive keeps its catalog and a page keeps its scroll position. One shared useDriveSession feeds Photos, Backups and Space; a phone-changed revision makes the grid reload after a delete elsewhere.
NOT YET

QR pairing and password protection.

Technical details
Planned: QR/manual-code pairing and auth for the phone-facing pages.

On your phone, plugged into a drive

No computer needed. The phone copies straight onto the drive.

WORKS

Plug in a drive and the first readable volume opens by itself in Devices; index it once, then browse it with full-screen zoom, video, and sharing. Its photos also appear in the unified Photos grid.

Technical details
Native Kotlin drive module (USB + SAF fallback): scan, hash, stat, mtime. Index/recover catalog, gallery grid, viewer. A volume that disappears and returns is allowed to auto-open again.
WORKS

Back up the camera roll onto the drive from Backups, in dated folders, choosing what counts as a duplicate and where duplicates go.

Technical details
Backup conditions, dated layout, content-based duplicate policy, custom quarantine folder constrained inside _OCD Duplicates. The open drive and its free space come from the shared drive session.
WORKS

Duplicates are compared by what is inside the file, not by its name. The redundant copy is moved to a duplicates folder, never deleted.

Technical details
Verified duplicate hashing, collision-safe catalog commit, quarantine replacement, audit row per move. Covered by mobile/scripts/catalog-merge.test.mjs.
WORKS

Clean-up stays honest if you cancel halfway or something fails: the index and the record still agree with what is on the drive.

Technical details
Catalog + audit commit after cancellation and partial failure; truncated/unreadable scan, unknown free space, read-only drive, and orphan staging handled explicitly.
WORKS

See the size of the job before it starts. The backup panel counts what will be copied, shows the drive’s room now and after, and says that the phone itself stays unchanged. If the drive is known to be too full, it says so before copying anything.

Technical details
Pure arithmetic in mobile/src/direct/spacePlan.ts (count, bytes, skipped, remaining room, free after delete) with node tests; estimateBackup applies the same rules as the run without hashing or writing; 64 MiB headroom; unknown free space is shown as unknown, never zero.
WORKS

Pick just the photos you want. Choose exact photos and videos instead of backing up by rule.

Technical details
Camera-roll picker with paging and multi-select passes a BackupSelection of asset ids to runBackup; duplicate policy, dated layout, hashing, read-back verification, catalog merge and audit all run unchanged.
WORKS

Live Photos stay together, and photos keep their own date, camera and place. A Live Photo’s video is copied beside its photo, and the backup reads capture date, orientation, camera, exposure and GPS from the file itself.

Technical details
iOS pairedVideoAsset copied to the photo’s final stem and verified with its own hash; both copies roll back if either fails. Android same-stem sidecars linked during catalog commit. EXIF from CoreImage (iOS) and flat tag maps (Android), including fractions, GPS refs and 0,0; stored in the catalog and preserved by adoption and rewrites.
WORKS

Backup keeps going with the screen locked on Android, and an interrupted run can be continued. A progress notification stays in the shade, and if the system kills the app the backup screen offers to pick up the same run on the same drive.

Technical details
Android foreground service (dataSync) with a low-importance notification and partial wake lock; START_NOT_STICKY, no restart without JavaScript. A resume marker in the app’s document folder records the drive, rules and ticked ids; a resumed run forces the duplicate policy to skip and rewrites the catalog if the previous kill happened during the catalog commit. iOS runs in the foreground only.

Keeping things safe

WORKS

Backing up never deletes or changes the photos on your phone.

WORKS

A duplicate is decided by the file’s content, so the wrong drive file is never the one moved.

WORKS

Nothing is hard-deleted. Redundant copies go to a duplicates folder and can be put back from the record.

WORKS

Unreadable scans, drives with no free-space figure, and read-only drives are refused or handled read-only instead of guessed at.

WORKS

Every destructive step is written down before it reports success.
